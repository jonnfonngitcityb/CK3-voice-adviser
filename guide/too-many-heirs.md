# Decision

I want one heir to hold the main title. What do disinherit, the church, a title law, or a murder change?

# Applies when

Feudal government, and the realm law is still a partition. If the law is already Primogeniture or Ultimogeniture, younger children do not inherit titles. This page is for the split you can still prevent in 867.

Depends on `realm-splits-on-death.md` for which law you can pass. Read that page only if he asks which law is reachable.

# Do this

1. Open Realm → Succession and name the player heir and every title a younger heir is about to take.
2. Pick one action that removes a person or a title from that list. Then look at the preview again. Do not stack three remedies before the preview moves.

Actions that exist:

- Disinherit, on the character interaction, and only if you are the dynasty head. The tooltip shows the Renown and Prestige. The Disinherited trait blocks that character and their descendants when the person who dies is the same dynasty. It also costs Legitimacy.
- Ask to Take Vows, if the faith has the Monasticism tenet. The interaction is on that character. A Monk or Nun does not inherit. Their children still can, and the monk still inherits claims.
- Add Feudal Elective to the kingdom or a duchy. You must be at peace. It costs 1500 Prestige for that title. It is allowed for a feudal ruler who does not have the Witenagemot, Ting-Meet, or Jirga tradition. French qualifies. Electors are the title holder, de jure vassals, and direct vassals one or two ranks below the title. A hook can force a vote.
- Murder is a hostile scheme. Success kills the target. It does not remove the target's heirs.

# What each choice changes

- Disinherit one younger son. He and his line drop out of your succession. The titles he was going to take are divided again among whoever is left. You spend Renown, Prestige, and Legitimacy. Compassionate and Forgiving characters gain Stress from it. He can still be a claimant in politics if he already has a claim. Check the preview.
- Ask to Take Vows. That one person leaves the line. His children stay in it. If the faith screen has no Monasticism tenet, the interaction is not there.
- Feudal Elective on the primary kingdom. Electors choose who gets that title, including a claimant or a valid elector, not only your oldest son. Your realm law still divides everything the elective does not cover. The Succession article (verified for 1.10) says the capital county follows the realm-law heir, not the elected heir, when those are different people. Read the preview before you spend the 1500 Prestige.
- Murder a younger heir. He dies if it succeeds. His own heir takes his place in the partition. If the scheme is discovered, the chance is the missing Secrecy. Being at war gives Murder −50 Success Chance. You gain Stress if you are Honest, Just, or Compassionate.

# Suggested play

For an 867 king, elective on the one kingdom is the change that can keep that crown on one person without waiting for Late Medieval. Read who the electors are before you add it. Disinherit only the child the preview still shows taking a kingdom after that. Do not murder an heir who has sons. The sons replace him.

# Why

A title succession law is a vote on that title only. Realm succession still runs on every other title. Candidates for Feudal Elective are a legitimate close family member who is not in line for a theocracy or a republic, a claimant who is not in that line, and a valid elector. Children of a candidate cannot be nominated.

Removing the elective costs Prestige that scales with the title's rank.

Disinherited characters do not inherit claims either, as long as the predecessor is the same dynasty. A monk does not disinherit his descendants.

# If this does not match

If Disinherit is missing, you are not the dynasty head, or the tooltip names the real block. If Feudal Elective is missing, the culture has Witenagemot, Ting-Meet, or Jirga, or you are at war. If Take Vows is missing, the faith has no Monasticism tenet.

The succession preview is the result. If it still shows a split, the action did not hit the title that splits.
