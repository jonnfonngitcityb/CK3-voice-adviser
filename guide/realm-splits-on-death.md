# Decision

The realm will split when I die. Which law is that, and which laws can an 867 feudal ruler actually reach?

# Applies when

Feudal government. Not clan, tribal, administrative, nomadic, or theocratic. Those laws are different and are not on this page.

A realm succession law is the law on the Realm screen. A title succession law is an elective added to one title. This page is the realm law. Electives are on `too-many-heirs.md`.

# Do this

1. Open Realm, then Succession. Read the law name and the preview of who gets which title.
2. If the law is Confederate Partition, look at the preview for a younger heir with a title of the same rank as your primary title. The Succession article, last verified for 1.10, says that heir does not stay your vassal. The preview is the check.
3. Pass a different law only if it is on the law list. Changing a feudal succession law costs 500 Prestige. Every powerful vassal must have a positive opinion of you, or be terrified, or be imprisoned. It cannot be changed during a power sharing.

Laws a feudal ruler can pass, and the gate for each:

- Confederate Partition. Innovation: Gavelkind. Crown authority: Autonomous Vassals is enough.
- Partition. Innovation: Hereditary Rule. Crown authority: at least Limited.
- High Partition. Innovation: Heraldry, or Visigothic Codes. Crown authority: at least Limited.
- Primogeniture or Ultimogeniture. Innovation: Primogeniture. Crown authority: at least High. The Primogeniture innovation is Late Medieval. A culture cannot enter Late Medieval before the year 1200.
- House Seniority. Innovation: Heraldry, or Table of Princes. Crown authority: at least High, unless the culture has Table of Princes.

Crown authority gates: Limited needs Plenary Assemblies. High and Absolute need Royal Prerogative. The level can be raised every 20 years. The tooltip shows the Prestige cost.

Absolute Crown Authority can designate the heir. That chooses who the primary heir is. It is not itself a single-heir law. Read the preview to see what younger children still take.

# What each choice changes

- Stay on Confederate Partition. On death the game can create new titles for younger heirs, then divide the land. The primary heir gets the primary title, the realm capital, and the direct de jure titles tied to it. The Succession article, last verified for 1.10, says a younger heir with an equal-rank title does not become a vassal, and that gold, men-at-arms, and artifacts go to the primary heir. The preview shows the titles.
- Partition, once Hereditary Rule and Limited Crown Authority are both true. Same division of titles you already hold. The 1.20 law does not say it creates new titles. Confederate Partition does.
- High Partition, once Heraldry or Visigothic Codes is discovered. The player heir inherits at least half the titles. Younger heirs still take the rest. Heraldry is High Medieval. A culture cannot enter High Medieval before 1050. Visigothic Codes is Aragonese, Basque, Catalan, or Occitan, not French.
- Primogeniture. One heir, the oldest eligible child, gets every title. Younger children get nothing but claims. Not reachable in 867. The innovation is locked until Late Medieval, year 1200, and the law also needs High Crown Authority.
- Designate heir under Absolute Crown Authority. The person you name becomes the primary heir. Other children still inherit if the law is a partition. Use it to pick which child is primary, then read the preview.

# Suggested play

In 867, do not spend the reign chasing Primogeniture. Open the succession preview. If a second kingdom is on it, that is the split that matters. Stop the game from being able to create that kingdom, or accept the split. Partition is the first law change that removes the "new titles" line, and only after Hereditary Rule exists.

# Why

Eligible children are set by the gender law. Male preference means a daughter inherits only if she has no eligible brother. Bastard, Disinherited, and Eunuch are ineligible, and so are their descendants. A Monk or Nun is ineligible, but their own children are not, and the monk still inherits claims.

Under every partition on the 1.20 page, children cannot be granted a title they do not already stand to inherit. The primary heir is who you play next, unless that person is outside your dynasty.

The Succession article, last checked for 1.10, adds the limit the 1.20 law page does not spell out: Confederate Partition creates a title only if you could have created it, and only at the same rank as the primary title. Confirm that on the preview before you rely on it.

House Seniority gives every title to the oldest eligible member of the house. Your child gets nothing unless that child is the oldest.

# If this does not match

The law name on Realm → Succession wins. If the law you want is missing, open Culture → Innovations and read the era year and the missing innovation. If the button to change the law is disabled, read its tooltip. Power sharing, a short powerful vassal, or the Prestige cost is the usual block.

If the government is not Feudal, this page does not apply.
