# Decision

I started as Charles the Bald in 867. What matters this year, and what changes if I am a vassal of a Karling instead?

# Applies when

Bookmark 867. Either you are Charles II the Bald, King of West Francia, or you are a feudal vassal of a Karling king. Catholic feudal. If the government on the screen is not Feudal, stop. Administrative, tribal, and clan rulers do not use this page.

System rules live on the other pages. This page only picks which decision matters at the start.

# Do this

1. Open Realm → Succession. Read the law and whether any younger heir is about to receive a kingdom.
2. If you are a king, open Realm and read Crown Authority, then the coronation law if it is on the same screen.
3. Look at the map for an army with a raid flag and no war banner. That is `vikings-at-the-border.md`. A faction alert is `faction-fired.md`.

Charles at this bookmark is King of West Francia, a Karling. His son Louis the Stammerer is Count of Bellac. Ludwig the German is King of East Francia. The succession screen, not this paragraph, names the player heir and the claims.

# What each choice changes

- You are Charles, and the preview shows a second kingdom. Confederate Partition can create titles for younger heirs. An heir who ends up with a title equal to yours does not stay your vassal, according to the Succession article last verified for 1.10. The preview is the check. What to do about the law is `realm-splits-on-death.md`. Do that before you conquer more kingdom-sized land.
- You are Charles, and the preview shows only duchies under one kingdom. The realm you hold now does not create a second king. A war that puts a creatable kingdom in your hands changes that. Check the preview after the war, not before.
- You are Charles and the coronation law says Uncrowned. You have −2.5 monthly Legitimacy, −25% Legitimacy gain, and −10 vassal opinion, and you cannot use a crown or regalia artifact. Holding a coronation raises the law. It does not change succession. The activity is the button on that law. Coronations is a game rule. If the law is absent, the rule is off and this penalty is not on you.
- You are a vassal of a Karling. Your liege's partition can give you a different liege when he dies. Your own counties still follow your law, on `realm-splits-on-death.md`. His claimant faction can put someone else over you. You do not control his crown authority unless you are in the faction that is demanding it.

# Suggested play

First year as Charles: read the succession preview, and answer a raid that is already on a holding. Do not form a second kingdom on purpose while the law is Confederate Partition. A coronation is the next act if the law says Uncrowned and no war is on the banner. As a Karling vassal, ignore his kingdom wars unless the faction window has your name in it.

# Why

West Francia in 867 is a feudal kingdom surrounded by other Karling kingdoms and by neighbors who can raid. The long-term history on the character, a later Italian crown and a line that loses the kingship in 987, is not a modifier. Nothing on this page gives you a claim you do not already see.

High Partition and Primogeniture are not this year's laws. The years and innovations are on `realm-splits-on-death.md`.

# If this does not match

If the ruler is not Charles and not a Karling's vassal, use the system page for the question you actually asked. The index line for this file is only the opening situation.

If Succession shows a law this page did not assume, the law on the screen wins, and `realm-splits-on-death.md` is the page that explains that law.
