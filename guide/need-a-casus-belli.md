# Decision

I want this title or this county. Which war goal can I use, and what does winning give me?

# Applies when

Feudal ruler declaring a war, or about to. The target is landed. This page covers a claim and a de jure war. Other goals appear only if Declare War lists them. Their tooltips are the rule. Holy-war doctrine costs are not written here.

# Do this

1. Select the target realm and open Declare War. Use a goal that is already on the list.
2. If the goal you want is missing, get the claim or the innovation below, then look again.
3. Read the goal's tooltip before you declare. It names the titles that move and who receives them.

How to get a claim:

- Your Realm Priest fabricates one. Catholic clerical tradition is theocratic, so the councillor is the Realm Priest, not the Chancellor. Set Fabricate Claim on County. At 100% you get an unpressed claim. Base speed is 3% a month, plus 0.2% a month per point of his Learning. A positive event can hand you a duchy claim instead. A negative event can cost Piety, anger the target, and cost vassal opinion.
- Children hold implicit claims on a parent's titles while the parent lives. The faith's view on gender decides which children. Male dominated means sons. On the parent's death, a child who does not inherit the title gets a pressed claim.
- A pressed claim is inherited by the holder's primary heir as an unpressed claim. An unpressed claim is not inherited. If you press an unpressed claim and the war ends in white peace, it becomes pressed.
- Claim Throne is a hostile scheme, not the priest's task. It needs the Meritocracy perk and you must be landed. Success gives a pressed claim on your liege's primary title.

De jure: holding a title gives a casus belli on the de jure titles under it. A de jure county war needs the Casus Belli innovation. A de jure duchy war needs Chronicle Writing.

# What each choice changes

- Press your own claim and enforce. You gain the claimed title. The tooltip names the rank. A claim on your own vassal's title can instead be used to revoke that title without tyranny, with no war.
- Press someone else's claim and enforce. They get the title. If they are a lower rank than you, they can come in as your vassal. The tooltip says whether they do. You do not keep their land in your domain.
- White peace after pressing an unpressed claim. You do not take the land. The claim becomes pressed, so your heir can inherit it as unpressed later.
- De jure war, enforced. The de jure land named on the tooltip moves. You do not get a claim on unrelated titles outside that de jure.
- Fabricate and wait. You are not at war yet. The priest can fail the task and anger the target. A finished county claim is unpressed until a white peace or a death turns it into something your heir can use.

# Suggested play

For an 867 feudal king, press a claim you already have before you spend months on the priest. Use the priest on one county that the succession preview is not already giving a son. Do not press a cousin's claim unless the tooltip says he becomes your vassal. Otherwise you won him a kingdom.

# Why

A claim is the right to a title you do not hold. Pressed and unpressed differ in inheritance, not in whether you may declare. Implicit claims exist only in the parent's lifetime.

The Chancellor in 1.20 does not fabricate claims. His tasks are Foreign Affairs, Domestic Affairs, Integrate Title, and Bestow Royal Favor. Older guides that send you to the Chancellor are wrong on this patch.

# If this does not match

Declare War is the list. If Fabricate Claim is not on the Realm Priest, your clerical tradition is lay clergy and the councillor is the Court Chaplain, who has the same task. If neither councillor shows it, read the council task list. Do not invent a third councillor.

If the de jure goal is missing, open Innovations and look for Casus Belli or Chronicle Writing.
