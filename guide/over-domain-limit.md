# Decision

I am over the domain limit. What do I give away, and what does that do under an early partition law?

# Applies when

Feudal government. The domain is the holdings you own yourself, not your vassals' land. A king who is merely over the two-duchy opinion cap is in the second half of this page, even if the domain limit itself is fine.

If the succession law is Confederate Partition, the gift can change what gets created on death. Then also open `realm-splits-on-death.md`.

# Do this

1. Open the domain number on your realm or character screen. Holdings past the limit stop paying taxes and levies after a year.
2. Give away a holding you are allowed to give. Under Partition, High Partition, and Confederate Partition, you cannot grant a child a title they do not already stand to inherit.
3. If you are a king, count your duchies. Two are free. Three are free if one of them is a head-of-faith title. Each duchy past that is −15 opinion with all vassals.

The domain limit for a feudal ruler starts from the game rule, then adds:

- +3 if your primary title is a kingdom. A duke or count gets +2. An emperor gets +4.
- +1 for every 6 points of Stewardship.
- Whatever else the domain tooltip lists. Innovations, perks, and artifacts on that tooltip count. The tooltip is the full list.

# What each choice changes

- Grant a county to the younger son the preview already gives it to. It leaves your domain now, so the over-limit penalty can stop. He is your vassal if the title is a lower rank than yours. On death he was going to take it anyway. The Succession article (verified for 1.10) says the other heirs then do not get claims on a title you already handed to the heir who was going to receive it. Confirm on the preview after the grant.
- Grant a county to a vassal who is not an heir. You can do this when you are not blocked by the "does not stand to inherit" rule. You lose the holding's taxes and levies forever, not only for the year of penalty. Under Confederate Partition, losing counties can also drop you below the size that would create a second kingdom on death. Check the succession preview after the grant.
- Keep the extra holding. For a year it still pays. After that it pays no taxes and no levies. You still own it, and it still counts toward what succession can hand out.
- Destroy or avoid a third duchy as a king. The −15 opinion per extra duchy comes off. The counties inside can stay with you or with a vassal. Destroying a title costs Prestige. The title screen shows the cost. This does not by itself fix the domain limit.

# Suggested play

As an 867 king, give the preview's younger son the county he is already going to inherit, if that county is what put you over the limit. Then look at the succession preview again. If giving land away removed a kingdom the game was about to create, that was the better gift. Do not hand a random courtier your capital county. The capital goes to the primary heir and is the holding you keep.

# Why

The penalty is not immediate. Newly controlled holdings over the limit stop providing taxes and levies after a year. Being one over and being five over are the same rule on this page. The tooltip shows if a harsher step applies in your game.

Overextension, a separate cap of 30 counties in the realm, hits counts and dukes. It does not use this king's duchy cap. Each county past 30 cuts gold income by 5%. A king uses the two-duchy rule instead.

# If this does not match

The domain tooltip's limit wins over the +3 and the Stewardship math here. If a grant is refused, the button's reason wins. The usual partition reason is that this child does not stand to inherit that title.

If the government is not feudal, the limit formula on this page does not apply.
